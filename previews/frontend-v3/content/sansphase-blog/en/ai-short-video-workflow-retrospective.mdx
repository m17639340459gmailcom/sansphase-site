> Fictional demonstration · Quiet Harbor, its storyboard, and viewer reactions are invented for this preview.

Quiet Harbor is an imagined harbor exercise. One shot shows the light offshore, a second brings the boat alongside a quay, and the third indicates its bearing. The three boards looked consistent side by side. When played in sequence with sound, the reviewer read the second shot as the boat moving out rather than in.

## Begin with frames that can be compared

Give each frame one spatial relationship and write down the reference the adjacent frame needs to keep. The first board leaves the light in position. The second includes that same light and the quay edge. The third brings in the bearing. A similar horizon height across the boards saves the viewer from searching for a new reference after each cut.

## Bring in the direction before the line ends

In the draft, the arrow appeared after the narrator's final words, while the boat was already leaving the frame. The revised sample introduces it halfway through the line and holds the boat and bearing together until the caption clears. Watch at normal speed first. On a second pass, pause only to mark what you can locate, such as a caption missing two beats into the boat's entrance.

## Track the questions still open

The sample covers a phone display with mono narration. Spacing on a big screen, understanding without audio, and color transitions in the complete export all need separate reviews. The handoff includes the current board number and the interval where each open question belongs.

### What gets passed on

The demonstration leaves three boards, a review clip, and one issue to investigate. The next reviewer can begin at the second shot without assembling the whole experiment again.
