> Fictional demonstration · The project, people, and observations in this note are invented.

Tide Archive began as one folder for a short harbor film. It held asset notes, meeting fragments, and two rounds of revisions. A reviewer could open every file but could not explain a decision the previous round had settled. Why had the opening moved from a wide shot in fog to a closer shot with the quay edge in frame? This walkthrough uses the same fictional records to recover the answer.

## Start with the handoff question

The reviewer had three tasks: find the current opening, explain the change, and determine whether the other framing was still under consideration. On the first pass, they found storyboard revision seven but treated the rejected wide shot as an alternative. Both files said “opening” and “fog.” What was missing was a clear indication of which record held the current decision.

| Record | What it holds | What it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project index | Current revision and handoff point | Where should a new reviewer start? |
| Source card | Quotation, record ID, and scope | Why was the shot changed? |
| Change log | Previous decision and reason for replacement | What changed after review? |

## Link the decision to its source

The new decision card says to reveal the quay outline earlier. It points to a fictional screening note, where the reviewer says the original shot kept the harbor hard to recognize until the narration had finished. The card includes the boundary: this check applies to the opening and says nothing about the palette for the full film. A reader can go straight from the decision to the relevant section of the source.

## Run the search again and save the gap

The second question used different words: “Why do we see the shoreline earlier?” This time the project index led to the current framing, and the source card explained why. One gap remained. A query for “cover” still pulled in two poster records as well. The next round would add a scope tag and use the unsuccessful query as a control.

### What the next person receives

The demo hands over the project index, four source cards, and one unresolved note. Start from the current decision and record a missing source when the record gives no answer.
