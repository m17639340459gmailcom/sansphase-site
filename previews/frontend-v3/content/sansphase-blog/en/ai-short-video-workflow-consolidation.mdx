> Fictional demonstration · First Light, Homeward, and their production outcomes are sample content.

Both test-screening notes mentioned rushed captions. Looking closer revealed different causes. In First Light the narration was still describing a light change when the caption moved to the next shot. In Homeward a moving shot carried the instructions for the boat's route. Keeping the observations separate made it possible to return each issue to the right stage.

## Hand one version to the next stage

Use a handoff sheet for each sample clip, with fields for the script revision, audio to retain, focal subjects, and screening notes. Check the sheet whenever the narration is recorded again. A duration change can otherwise leave the edit and captions following an older recording. In the sample for Homeward, a voice revision ran half a line longer, so the new shot boundary was checked before the subtitle line break.

## Review the densest passage early

The most demanding section placed a lit boat, a marker, and a route caption on screen together. First review a low-resolution export and see whether the subject is still visible when the caption has been read. The marker can enter later and the main outline can stay in a stable position. The other shots retain their pacing.

## Check the full export at the end

After the sample passes, open the complete exported file. Check both ends and the audio, then watch how the previous scene enters the revised shot and how the revised shot hands off to the next one. If only one caption's timing changed, focus on the affected interval and both edges. Existing evidence still describes the untouched clips.

### What the next production would test

Try a voiced storyboard before preparing the final images. Record where the voice changes and what the viewer needs to recognize at the same time. Keep the problematic clip so a later review can compare when the new method catches the issue.
